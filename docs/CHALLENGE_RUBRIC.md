# Script Review — challenge-case rubric

These are original, visible development cases. Expected labels are not model results and are never supplied to inference. Test new unseen cases separately before claiming generalisation.

For each run check: (1) correct character and event, (2) open versus resolved, (3) evidence actually supports the exact question, (4) missing or extra hooks. A category match alone does not prove semantic correctness.

## Hard 01 · Identity subject trap

Expected open categories: identity.

Question must concern the ferryman possibly being Admiral Orrin. Lord Caden is the listener, not the mystery person.

## Hard 02 · Offer answered without yes

Expected open categories: none.

Acceptance is established by unambiguous action and dialogue. Do not invent an unresolved decision.

## Hard 03 · Emotional reaction is not acceptance

Expected open categories: decision.

Whether Liora accepts is open. A smile and packed bag do not establish acceptance here.

## Hard 04 · Past refusal, new offer

Expected open categories: decision.

The village job offer remains open. The refusal answered the earlier northern-city offer only.

## Hard 05 · False alarm explained

Expected open categories: none.

The apparent danger is explicitly explained and resolved. Do not retain a bomb/survival hook.

## Hard 06 · Escape still unconfirmed

Expected open categories: danger.

Neri’s rescue remains unresolved. Arrival of rescuers does not establish escape.

## Hard 07 · Two distinct open hooks

Expected open categories: identity, decision.

Separate prisoner-identity and Mira-acceptance questions. Do not merge them into one compound question.

## Hard 08 · Dramatic mood, no concrete hook

Expected open categories: none.

Ominous music and distant weather alone do not establish a concrete immediate danger, offer or identity clue.

## Hard 09 · Known to viewer, unknown to character

Expected open categories: none.

The viewer already knows the identity. Character ignorance is not an unresolved viewer identity question.

## Hard 10 · Narration within dialogue

Expected open categories: identity.

A fictional play does not confirm the real rider’s identity. Ground the real-world question in the signet and explicit uncertainty.
